Customer segmentation with k-means

This is a data analysis project on a retail dataset: reading, cleaning and exploring the data, then using the k-means clustering algorithm to segment customers by recency, frequency and spend.

1. Loading the data

I used a dataset named OnlineRetail.csv, which holds transaction data from an online retail store. The goal was to explore the dataset, clean it, visualize the important patterns and segment the customers.

2. Visualizing the data

After removing the null values, I created two extra columns, month and day of week, from the invoice date. The aim was to see whether the day or the month showed any pattern. The charts below show trends and distributions.

The bar plots show no transactions on Saturday. November had the most transactions, but total spend was highest in January.

These visuals also show the top five countries and the most purchased stock items.

3. Recency, frequency and spend

RFM analysis is a behaviour-based technique that segments customers by their transaction history. Recency is the number of days since a customer's last purchase, frequency is the number of transactions per customer, and spend is the total amount a customer spent.

last_transaction_date = df.groupby('CustomerID')['InvoiceDate'].max()
reference_date = max(df['InvoiceDate'])
days_difference = (reference_date - last_transaction_date).dt.days
days_difference = days_difference.reset_index().rename(columns={'InvoiceDate': 'recency'})

I merged these metrics into a single dataframe.

Boxplots show the outliers in each distribution.

The outliers were mostly in the frequency and spend columns. I removed them before applying k-means.

4. Scaling the features

With the outliers removed, I scaled the features so that no single metric dominates the clustering.

from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
X = rfs.iloc[:, 1:]
scaler = StandardScaler()
X = scaler.fit_transform(X)

5. Clustering with k-means

I used the Yellowbrick library to plot the k-means distortion score and find the elbow. The elbow sits at 4, which points to four clusters.

I then fitted k-means and added the cluster labels to the recency, frequency and spend dataframe.

kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=4, n_init='auto', random_state=42)
kmeans.fit(X)

6. Results

These are the four clusters. Recency is days since the last purchase, so a high value means the customer has not bought recently.

  • Cluster 0: high recency, low frequency, low spend
  • Cluster 1: low recency, high frequency, moderate spend
  • Cluster 2: low recency, low frequency, low spend
  • Cluster 3: low recency, high frequency, high spend
